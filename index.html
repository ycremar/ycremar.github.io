About

I build LLMs for search and recommendation systems at scale with low latency. My research also focuses on agentic AI: building and evaluating LLM agents through post-training, reward modeling, and agent harness design, and building benchmarks whose environments keep evolving. My recent work asks how agents hold up when the world changes, and whether they can faithfully simulate real multi-turn human behavior.

During my Ph.D., I worked on self-supervised learning, graph neural networks, and model explainability, with applications in AI4Science and scalable system in industry. Self-supervised learning has a model build its own training signal from raw data, so it scales with data rather than annotation, and that recipe underlies today's foundation models.

I hold a Ph.D. in Computer Science from Texas A&M University and a B.S. in Statistics from the School of the Gifted Young at the University of Science and Technology of China. I have served as an Area Chair for Language and Molecules @ ACL and Scaling Environments for Agents @ NeurIPS, and on the program committees of several conferences.
